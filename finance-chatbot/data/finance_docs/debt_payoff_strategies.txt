Debt Payoff Strategies: Snowball vs Avalanche

When paying down multiple debts (credit cards, personal loans, etc.), two
widely discussed strategies are the debt snowball and the debt avalanche.
Both assume you pay the minimum on every debt, then direct any extra money
toward one target debt at a time.

Debt avalanche:
Extra payments go toward the debt with the highest interest rate first,
regardless of balance size. Once that debt is paid off, the extra payment
rolls onto the debt with the next-highest rate, and so on. Mathematically,
this method minimizes the total interest paid over time, making it the most
efficient option in pure dollar terms.

Debt snowball:
Extra payments go toward the debt with the smallest balance first,
regardless of interest rate. Once the smallest debt is paid off, that
payment amount rolls onto the next-smallest balance. This method tends to
produce faster "wins" — a debt fully paid off sooner — which can build
motivation and momentum, even though it may cost somewhat more in total
interest compared to the avalanche method.

Choosing between them:
The avalanche method is the better choice on paper for minimizing interest
cost. The snowball method can be a better fit for someone who benefits from
early psychological wins to stay motivated and consistent. Since consistency
matters more than optimality if a plan doesn't get followed, the "best"
method is often whichever one a person will actually stick with.

Other general debt considerations:
- High-interest debt (many credit cards, some personal loans) usually
  deserves priority over low-interest debt (some mortgages, some student
  loans), since the cost of carrying it is higher.
- Consolidating multiple high-interest debts into a single lower-interest
  loan can reduce total interest, but only helps if it doesn't lead to
  running up new debt on the now-empty credit cards.
- Making at least the minimum payment on every debt, on time, protects your
  credit score while you focus extra payments on your target debt.
